## 1. Cryptid census (2025–2026)

Source of truth: [Cryptids page](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Cryptids). The community splits cryptids into **discovered** (found by census) and **constructed** (built to encode a known open problem). No naturally-discovered cryptid has been resolved. Current roster:

**Discovered cryptids:**

- **Bigfoot** — BB(3,3), found Oct/Nov 2023 by S. Ligocki ([wiki](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Bigfoot)). Return map on triples (a,b,c) driven by b mod 6 with a floor-affine rule; halts iff it reaches b ≡ 4 (mod 12) with a = 0. Modeled as a ±1 random walk on `a` (+1 w.p. 2/3); simulated past 24M rule steps (a ≈ 4·10^6); halting probability estimated 2^(−3,999,889). "Probviously non-halting." Compiled to a BB(7) 2-symbol machine (Iijil, 2024).
- **Hydra** — BB(2,5), found May 2024 by Daniel Yuan ([wiki](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Hydra), [sligocki's "BB(2,5) is Hard"](https://www.sligocki.com/2024/05/10/bb-2-5-is-hard.html)). Exactly the map we know: H(n) = floor(3n/2) bookkept as C(a,b) with even steps b+2, odd steps b−1, halt at b = −1; equivalently iterate f(n) = 3·floor(n/2)+3 from 3. 4M rule steps simulated (b ≈ 2·10^6); non-halt probability ≈ 1 − 10^(−419,099). A **"bonus" second BB(2,5) cryptid** with the same mechanism exists. Compiled to BB(9).
- **Antihydra** — BB(6), found June 2024 by mxdys ([wiki](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Antihydra), [sligocki's "BB(6) is Hard"](https://www.sligocki.com/2024/07/06/bb-6-2-is-hard.html)). Same H(n) = floor(3n/2) from 8 with reversed halting condition (halt iff odd steps ever exceed twice the even steps). Simulated to ~2^31 map steps (memory-bound; see [forum thread](https://discuss.bbchallenge.org/t/antihydra-simulation-status/242)); wiki reports halt probability ~10^(−28,723,042,565). Partial results: **Lean proofs** of equivalence to D(n+1) = ceil(3D(n)/2) ([rwst/Antihydra-Basics](https://github.com/rwst/Antihydra-Basics/)), a connection to **Mahler's Z-numbers** (3/2-equidistribution), and a proof it does **not** generate Sturmian words. An August 2025 near-linear-time simulation algorithm exists but memory grows linearly — the acknowledged bottleneck.
- **Space Needle** — BB(6), found Jan 9, 2025 by mxdys ([wiki](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Space_Needle)); TM `1RB1LA_1LC0RE_1LF1LD_0RB0LA_1RC1RE_---0LD`. High-level rules derived **July 31, 2025 by Racheline and Katelyn Doucette**: with v(b) the 2-adic valuation, from b = 6 iterate b -> b + v(b) + (3/2)(b/2^v(b) − 1); **halt iff b is a power of 2**. Equivalently (BMO form): for b = (2a+1)·2^k, f(b) = b + k + 3a; does the orbit of 6 hit 2^n? ([BMO problem 6](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/BMO)). Simulated to 17M+ terms; max 2-adic valuation observed is 24; LegionMammal978's random-lower-bits model gives log-scale random-walk growth. This is precisely the "orbit avoids powers of q" archetype, and the community explicitly labels it beyond current reach. **No invariant search (automata or otherwise) is documented for it.**
- **Lucy's Moonlight** — BB(6), found Mar 1, 2025 by Racheline ([wiki](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Lucy%27s_Moonlight)). The flagship **"probviously halting"** cryptid: behaves like repeated independent trials with fixed success probability, expected to halt at an astronomically large but finite time (c2 ≈ 10^2901); halting formulas by Racheline and S. Ligocki (Apr 2025), transition mechanics verified in Lean.
- **Fenrir** — **new, Mar 22, 2026**: the first **FRACTRAN cryptid**, found during the BBf(22) cleanup by **Jason Yuen and Claude Opus 4.6** ([wiki](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Fenrir)). Three size-22 programs, e.g. `[1/15, 27/77, 49/3, 10/49, 33/2]`, all equivalent to the 2-counter guarded walk: S(x,2y) -> S(x−1, 5y+2); S(x,2y+1) -> S(x+2, 5y); halt at S(0, even). This is *literally a 2-variable guarded affine counter machine* of our class.
- **April 2026**: a new BB(6) cryptid "similar to Space Needle" found by Discord user sheep (`1RB1LA_0LC0RC_1LE1RD_1RE1RC_1LF0LA_---1LE`), classification in progress ([TMBR April 2026](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/TMBR:_April_2026)). BB(3,3) additionally has "Wily Coyote"-type potential cryptids among its 6 remaining holdouts ([BB(3,3)](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/BB(3,3))).

**Constructed cryptids:** RH at BB(744), ZF-consistency at BB(432) (2025, Wade), **PA-consistency at BB(372) (2026, LegionMammal978)**, Goldbach at BB(25) (verified in Lean), weak Collatz at BB(124)/BB(43,4) (Stérin), Erdős's 2^n-base-3 conjecture at BB(5,4)/BB(15) (Stérin–Woods), plus Feb 2026 machines independent of PA and of ZFC+subtle cardinals ([TMBR Feb 2026](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/TMBR:_February_2026)). Status changes: only the (constructed, historical) FLT machine is "resolved" (by Wiles, not by machine analysis); in Mar 2026 DrDisentangle settled one *informal* cryptid as non-halting.

**Transfer:** our 4-variable system and the Hydra-conjugate both match the "discovered cryptid" template exactly; Fenrir shows the community now recognizes counter-machine cryptids as first-class. The census has no systematic slot for guarded affine counter machines with 2–4 variables — an open lane (see item 6).

## 2. BB(5) / BB(6) status

- **BB(5) = 47,176,870** is proved and formally verified in Coq/Rocq ([Coq-BB5](https://github.com/ccz181078/Coq-BB5)); the paper "Determination of the fifth Busy Beaver value" ([arXiv:2509.12337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12337)) was **accepted to STOC 2026** ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3798129.3806376)). It enumerates 181,385,789 machines; Coq-BB5 also establishes BB(2,4) = 3,932,964 and re-proves BB(4), BB(3), BB(2), BB(2,3). Separate decider write-up: "Turing machines deciders, part I" ([arXiv:2504.20563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20563)).
- **BB(6)**: champion by mxdys (June 2025) gives **S(6) > 2↑↑↑5** ([BB(6) wiki](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/BB(6))). Holdout counts (formal): 1,326 (Dec 2025) -> 1,214 (Feb 2026) -> 1,161 (Mar 2026) -> **~1,104 (Apr 2026)**, shrinking ~5%/month via FAR (Andrew Ducharme, mxdys), translated cyclers, "Cascade" accelerated simulation (prurq), "longitudinal acceleration" (mxdys), and equivalence-class merging (-d). All holdouts simulated ≥ 10^13 steps. Among BB(6) cryptids: roughly 8 probviously non-halting, 10 probviously halting, 1 genuinely uncertain (counts drift). mxdys's **new FAR variant solved 113 of 1,534 holdouts** on first deployment. Notably, in **April 2026 a BB(6) machine was proved non-halting using the Baker–Wüstholz theorem** (linear forms in logarithms), with formalization assisted by Claude Opus and Aristotle AI ([TMBR April 2026](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/TMBR:_April_2026)) — the first genuinely number-theoretic decider event, directly relevant to our multiplicative machines.
- Adjacent: BB(3,3) is down to 6 holdouts (4 up to equivalence), conditional on Bigfoot; three informal results formalized in Rocq Mar 15, 2026. BB(2,5) is at 69 formal holdouts, conditional on Hydra. BB(7) and BB(2,6) reductions ongoing.

**Transfer:** the field is now bottlenecked exactly on our problem class (Collatz-like orbit avoidance), and its monthly cadence ("TMBR") makes it easy to track. The Baker–Wüstholz event is the single most transferable 2026 development for our multiplicative machines.

## 3. Decider technology — and the key question

The umbrella framework is **CTL (Closed Tape Language)** ([wiki](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Closed_Tape_Language)): a certificate is a set A of configurations that (i) contains the initial configuration, (ii) is **closed under one TM step**, (iii) contains no halting configuration. All deciders below are instances with different presentation languages:

- **FAR (Finite Automata Reduction)** ([wiki stub](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Finite_Automata_Reduction); best source is the [decider README](https://github.com/bbchallenge/bbchallenge-deciders/tree/main/decider-finite-automata-reduction) and §6 of the BB(5) paper). Certificate = a DFA scanning the tape left of the head plus an NFA from the head rightward; phrased as **co-CTL**: the recognized language over-approximates "configurations from which halting is possible," must be closed under *predecessor*, and must exclude the initial configuration. **Search**: (a) `direct` — compute the NFA inductively as a closure (a quotient construction via left syntactic equivalence, in the style of Bouajjani–Esparza–Maler); (b) `mitm_dfa` — fix two DFAs (left tape, right tape), encode the closure conditions as **SAT**. Certificates stored in a standard binary format (DeciderType 10/11) with an independent verifier ([bbchallenge-nfa-verification](https://github.com/UncombedCoconut/bbchallenge-nfa-verification)).
- **WFAR / MITMWFAR** ([wiki stub](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/MITMWFAR); [Iijil1/MITMWFAR](https://github.com/Iijil1/MITMWFAR)). Adds **integer weights** to the two automata: each tape half maps to (state, accumulated weight); a configuration is accepted iff the weight **sum lies in an interval** attached to the (TM state, symbol, left state, right state) tuple. Decides **provably irregular machines** that no regular-language decider can touch. Search: enumerate DFA pairs closed under TM transitions, then try ±1 weightings.
- **RepWL (Repeated Word List)** ([wiki](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Repeated_Word_List)): tape as blocks with exponents, counts ≥ T abstracted to "T+" (regex-like `w^T+`); a counter-abstraction — can only certify behavior expressible with threshold-plus-periodicity conditions.
- **n-gram CPS** ([wiki](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/NGram_CPS)): local window around the head plus sets of boundary n-grams (Nathan Fenner; Rocq implementation by mxdys, Apr 2024). Coarser regular abstraction, cheap and effective in bulk.
- **Halting Segment** ([wiki](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Halting_Segment)): backward reasoning from next-step-halting configurations restricted to a fixed tape window (Iijil, 2022).
- **Bouncers / Translated Cyclers** ([Bouncer wiki](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Bouncer)): certify self-similar sweep patterns via shift rules and an inductive bounce rule; specific dynamical shapes, not invariants.

### Regular invariants for arithmetic maps (the key question)

**Empirical status quo:** Every cryptid has survived FAR, WFAR, RepWL, CPS — that's what "holdout" means. But all deciders operate on **tapes encoding counters in unary/block form**. A regular language over unary-coded counters can only constrain each counter by **ultimately periodic (congruence + threshold) conditions** — the semilinear/Parikh collapse of regular sets over one-letter alphabets. So the observed failure of regular deciders on Hydra-type machines is *predicted exactly by our no-congruence theorems*. Publishable observation: our theorems are the first formal explanation of *why* these machines are irregular in the bbchallenge sense (asserted empirically, never structurally, in the wiki and papers).

**The base-q loophole is real and untried.** Powers of 2 = `10*` is base-2 regular; the one-step relations of these maps are base-2 **synchronized-automatic**: y = floor(3x/2) is definable in Büchi arithmetic (N, +, V2) — exactly the logic **Walnut** decides ([Mousavi, arXiv:1603.06017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06017); [Shallit's Walnut book](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~shallit/walnut-book.html)). For any *given* automaton R, "R is regular, 6 in R, f(R) ⊆ R, R ∩ 10* = ∅" is a **decidable, push-button check**. Nobody in either community has run this search on Space Needle, Hydra/Antihydra, or any cryptid map. Closest existing work:

- **Shallit–Wilson 1992** ("The 3x+1 problem and finite automata," Bull. EATCS 46): the set of t-step Collatz ancestors of a fixed target is base-2 regular (doubly-exponential DFA; singly exponential via [Stérin's "Binary expression of ancestors in the Collatz graph"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00775)). Finite-horizon only; no invariants.
- **Stérin–Woods**, ["The Collatz process embeds a base conversion algorithm"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.06979) — the rewriting/automata view underlying their BB(15) construction.
- **Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule**, ["An Automated Approach to the Collatz Conjecture"](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10817-022-09658-8) (CADE'21/JAR 2023; [PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~mheule/publications/collatz.pdf), ["Mixed Base Rewriting"](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~mheule/publications/WST21.pdf)): SAT-driven matrix/arctic interpretations (weighted-automata-style certificates) proving termination of weakenings — the only serious precedent for SAT-searched algebraic certificates on Collatz digit strings, but on the **termination** side; cryptid work needs the **non-halting/invariant** side. Shared technology with FAR's `mitm_dfa`.
- **Regular model checking**: length-preserving transducers, acceleration/widening to find regular inductive invariants ([Esparza et al., "Regular Model Checking Upside-Down," arXiv:2205.03060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03060); [MFCS 2025 effectively regular reachability](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18833)). Never pointed at a Collatz-like map — the RMC community verifies protocols, not arithmetic.

**Assessment (agent's analysis, not found written anywhere):** for the *Hydra map* x -> floor(3x/2), a nontrivial base-2 regular invariant is plausibly **impossible** — multiplication by 3/2 mixes base-2 digits ergodically; Cobham / Furstenberg ×2-×3 rigidity heuristics suggest any 2-recognizable set closed under a ×3/2-like map must be trivial. But that cuts both ways and is precisely the opportunity: **either** a SAT search finds a small certificate (sensational), **or** we prove a "no-regular-invariant" theorem — the strict base-q strengthening of our no-congruence theorems, the first *structural* explanation of cryptid irregularity. Space Needle is the best first target: halting set maximally regular-friendly, map valuation-driven, both outcomes live. WFAR's lesson: adding Z-weights is what rescued irregular TMs — for counter machines, weighted automata over digit strings ≈ affine-guard structure, so a "weighted regular invariant" (interval-accept on a weight tracking e.g. a − 2b) is the right generalization when the plain regular one fails.

## 4. Certification practice

- Gold standard is **Rocq (Coq)**: [Coq-BB5](https://github.com/ccz181078/Coq-BB5) contains verified deciders run *inside* Coq, plus [busycoq](https://github.com/meithecatte/busycoq) (meithecatte) for hand-proved sporadics (Skelet #1, #17; cf. [Xu's Skelet #17 paper, arXiv:2407.02426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02426)). Trusted base = the Rocq kernel; decider verdicts re-derived by verified code or checked from **standardized certificate files** by small verifiers. BB(5) at STOC 2026 — formal certification is now a *publishing asset*.
- **Lean is fully accepted for cryptid mathematics**: Antihydra's TM<->map equivalence is formalized in Lean ([rwst/Antihydra-Basics](https://github.com/rwst/Antihydra-Basics/)); Goldbach BB(25) is Lean-verified; April 2026 saw Lean formalizations **with LLM assistance** normalized, plus the Baker–Wüstholz BB(6) non-halting proof done with Claude Opus and Aristotle AI. Mathlib has a Turing-machine formalization to build on.
- **Verdict on formalizing our theorems in Lean 4:** feasible and squarely within community practice. The community's standard first artifact for a cryptid is exactly what we have: verified acceleration (machine -> return map conjugacy) plus verified halting criterion. rwst/Antihydra-Basics is the direct template; our no-cycle and no-congruence theorems would go beyond anything formalized for their own cryptids. Natural vehicle for getting our machines *onto the wiki* as recognized cryptids.

## 5. "Probvious" and community epistemics

- **Probvious** ([wiki](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Probvious)) is Conway's coinage from "On Unsettleable Arithmetical Problems" (Amer. Math. Monthly, 2013). The community operationalizes it: model the accelerated map as a biased random walk, compute tail halting probability (Bigfoot 2^(−3,999,889); Hydra 10^(−419,099); Antihydra ~10^(−2.87e10)), tag machines accordingly. No formal writeup beyond Conway's article, blog posts ([sligocki](https://www.sligocki.com/2024/07/06/bb-6-2-is-hard.html)), and press ([Brubaker](https://benbrubaker.com/why-busy-beaver-hunters-fear-the-antihydra/), [Quanta Aug 2025](https://www.quantamagazine.org/busy-beaver-hunters-reach-numbers-that-overwhelm-ordinary-math-20250822/)).
- The **formal "as hard as X" tradition**: Yedidia–Aaronson (BB(748) independent of ZFC), Aaronson's ["Busy Beaver Frontier"](https://www.scottaaronson.com/papers/bb.pdf), and closest to us **Stérin–Woods, "Hardness of busy beaver value BB(15)"** ([arXiv:2107.12475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12475)): hardness as *reduction* ("knowing BB(15) would settle Erdős's 2^n base-3 conjecture"). The Erdős machine is structurally our problem: orbit of n -> 2n avoiding the base-3-regular set {no digit 2} — the community's only certified cryptid whose halting set is a *regular digit condition*.
- **Hardness ranking:** the community has only the three-way tag (probviously halting / non-halting / uncertain) plus informal "Hard" labels; Brubaker notes explicitly that for Antihydra "there isn't a more precise mathematical link" to Collatz. Our **certified-vs-candidate ranking with Pi-0-1/Pi-0-2 distinctions** has no counterpart and would likely be welcomed — natural venues: the wiki Cryptids cluster, discuss.bbchallenge.org, TMBR.

## 6. Counter machines, FRACTRAN, and existing censuses

- **FRACTRAN busy beaver (BBf)** ([wiki](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Fractran)): BBf(n) = max runtime from state 2 over halting programs of size n. Run by Jason Yuen (@-d), Daniel Yuan, Shawn Ligocki. **BBf(21) = 31,957,632** (Mar 2026); **BBf(22) > 1.146e62** with the census complete *except the three Fenrir programs* (Claude Opus 4.6 resolved 1,997 of 2,003 holdouts non-halting + 3 halting, Mar 2026); **BBf(23) > 4.393e124**, champion June 2026, **21,295 holdouts** open; further cryptids: "Frankenstein's Monster" and an "Antihydra-like" program at size 23. Public holdout lists: [int-y1/BBFractran](https://github.com/int-y1/BBFractran/blob/main/holdout/README.md); spacetime visualizer: [FractranVisualizer](https://github.com/Laturas/FractranVisualizer). FRACTRAN programs *are* multiplicative guarded counter machines; their deciders are "linear invariants and acceleration" — cruder than our q-adic valuation-independence theorems.
- **Register-machine busy beaver MBB(n)** ([wiki](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Register_machine)), introduced **Dec 2025**: Minsky machines, MBB = max instructions executed from zeros. Known: MBB(1..6) = 1, 3, 5, 10, 24, 49; lower bounds MBB(7) ≥ 231, MBB(8) ≥ 3,394, MBB(9) ≥ 9,870. **No cryptids by exhaustive search yet**, but a 10-instruction Hydra was hand-coded — the community expects counter-machine cryptids around size 8–10 and hasn't reached them. Our 2–4-variable guarded *affine* machines sit between MBB and FRACTRAN; **no census of guarded-affine counter machines exists** — we would not be duplicating anyone.
- Other model censuses (how such contributions land): BBλ for lambda calculus (Tromp; BBλ(38) = 5·2^2222+6 solved Apr 2026, 74-bit cryptid found; paper in *Entropy*), BBµ for general recursive functions (Ligocki, Dec 2025; BBµ(100) > Graham's number), [Instruction-Limited BB](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Instruction-Limited_Busy_Beaver), [Beeping BB](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/Beeping_Busy_Beaver), "Castor Ministerialis" ([arXiv:2510.17438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17438), Hercher). New model + census + cryptid is an established, welcomed genre.

## Ranked opportunities (agent's conclusion)

**1. Run the first automata-invariant hunt on an arithmetic cryptid map — Space Needle and our own machines — and, in parallel, attempt the no-regular-invariant theorem.** Base-2 representation; halting set `10*`; map synchronized-automatic; encode "DFA R with n states, 6 in L(R), step-closure, disjoint from 10*" as SAT exactly as FAR's `mitm_dfa`; sweep n = 2..~40; candidates machine-checkable in Walnut. Nobody — bbchallenge, RMC, or automatic-sequences — has tried this on a Collatz-like map. If search fails at small sizes, pivot to proving failure (Cobham/×2-×3 rigidity direction). Either outcome is strong.

**2. Port WFAR-style weighted certificates to counter machines directly, and join the BBf(23) / MBB censuses.** Adapt Iijil's MITMWFAR search to guarded affine rules; target-rich: 21,295 BBf(23) holdouts, and Fenrir — a 2-counter guarded walk our machinery applies to verbatim. Lowest-friction community entry: solve holdouts, get on TMBR, then propose the guarded-affine census as a new wiki model page (our machines as founding cryptids).

**3. Formalize acceleration + halting-criterion theorems in Lean 4 and publish as cryptid pages; pursue the Baker–Wüstholz route for the multiplicative machines.** The April 2026 Baker–Wüstholz non-halting proof of a BB(6) machine is proof-of-concept that linear-forms-in-logs can close cryptid-adjacent cases: for machines whose halting set is powers of q, Baker–Wüstholz gives effective lower bounds on |a log p − b log q| — potentially enough to prove orbit-avoidance for the thin affine families in our halting sets: a *certified partial resolution*, something no discovered cryptid currently has.

Caveats: wiki-derived figures drift monthly; FAR/WFAR math descriptions come from implementers' READMEs and the BB(5) paper; the Baker–Wüstholz item is so far documented only in TMBR April 2026.
